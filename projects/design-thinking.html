Re-imagining how students find a seat at Qingfen Canteen during peak hours — a design-thinking journey from “there aren't enough seats” to “you can't tell which seats are free.”

The Process

From assumption to insight

We followed the double-diamond — Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver — and let the observations overturn our first guess.

During peak hours, students at Qingfen spend a long time hunting for a seat. But most seats look taken — bags are draped over chairs, so it's impossible to tell whether a seat is truly free or just temporarily held. The canteen feels permanently overcrowded, and anyone in a hurry leaves frustrated and anxious.

01 · Our initial assumption

“It must be a bag-storage problem.”

If bags are taking up the chairs, give them somewhere to go — hooks, baskets, drawers, lockers. We sketched and prototyped storage fixes.

02 · First solution & testing

Storage didn't free the seats.

Even with somewhere to put their bags, students still occupied the chairs. The hook already existed in real canteens — and people barely used it. The problem clearly wasn't storage alone.

03 · Deeper observation

We started watching how people move.

Shadowing students from entry to first bite, mapping the “hunt,” and comparing high-density Qingfen against the quieter Halal canteen. Roughly 1 in 3 chairs was held by a bag or used as a social buffer — not a person.

04 · The problem, reframed

The real issue is visibility & uncertainty.

Students simply don't know which seats are available. A temporary absence looks exactly like a vacancy. The canteen needs a system that makes seat availability visible and trustworthy.

05 · The final solution

An intelligent, connected canteen.

Instead of relying on bags as informal markers, the canteen becomes one interrelated system: real-time monitoring detects whether seats are truly occupied, and a clear status tells students what's free — turning every arrival into a guaranteed spot.

System Design

Three parts, one experience

/ 01

Dynamic Monitoring

Detects real movement or presence at each seat — so a bag alone no longer counts as “occupied.”

/ 02

Seat Status Indicator

Shows real-time availability: free, occupied, or temporarily unattended — at a glance.

/ 03

Diverse Table Types

Solo-to-group seating with flexible configurations, so solo diners stop blocking 4-person tables.
