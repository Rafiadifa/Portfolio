The Intelligent Canteen

How do students find a seat at Qingfen Canteen when it's busy? Our design-thinking project started at “there aren't enough seats” and ended somewhere more useful: you can't tell which seats are actually free.

The Process

From assumption to insight

We worked through the double-diamond (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver), and the observations ended up overturning our first guess.

When it's busy, students at Qingfen spend a long time hunting for a seat. Most seats look taken, because bags are draped over the chairs, so you can't tell whether a seat is really free or just being held for a minute. The place feels permanently packed, and anyone in a hurry leaves annoyed.

01 · Our initial assumption

“It must be a bag-storage problem.”

If bags were taking up the chairs, the fix seemed obvious: give them somewhere else to go. We sketched storage ideas like hooks, baskets, and drawers, and prototyped a few.

02 · First solution & testing

Storage didn't free the seats.

Even with somewhere to put their bags, students still kept the chairs. Real canteens already have hooks, and people barely use them. So storage wasn't really the problem.

03 · Deeper observation

We started watching how people move.

We shadowed students from the door to their first bite, mapped the “hunt” for a seat, and compared crowded Qingfen with the quieter Halal canteen. About 1 in 3 chairs was taken by a bag or used as a social buffer, not a person.

04 · The problem, reframed

The real issue is visibility & uncertainty.

Students don't actually know which seats are open. Someone who stepped away for a minute looks exactly like an empty seat. What the canteen needs is a way to show availability that people can trust.

05 · The final solution

An intelligent, connected canteen.

Instead of leaning on bags as informal markers, the canteen works as one connected system. Sensors check whether a seat is really in use, and a clear status tells students what's open, so arriving means you actually get a seat.

System Design

Three parts, one experience

/ 01

Dynamic Monitoring

Reads real movement or presence at each seat, so a bag on its own no longer counts as taken.

/ 02

Seat Status Indicator

Shows each seat at a glance: free, occupied, or just left for a moment.

/ 03

Diverse Table Types

A mix of seating, from single spots to group tables, so one person stops blocking a table for four.
