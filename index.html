05 / About

About me

Architecture student at Tsinghua, drawn to buildings that keep people safe and cities that work for everyone.

I'm Rafi, an architecture student at Tsinghua. I came to architecture through drawing, but it turned serious after an earthquake one night back home in Indonesia. The next morning my school was wrecked, while our house barely noticed. It's a traditional stilt house from Kampung Naga, the village my grandfather's family comes from. I spent a high-school research project working out why it survived, and after that architecture stopped being a hobby and became a question: how do you build things that keep people safe?

At Zijing College I landed in a Design Science track that let me lean toward urban planning and landscape architecture. These days I'm pulled two ways: earthquake-resilient structures rooted in Kampung Naga, and the messier question of how a city like Jakarta grows, who it works for, and who it leaves behind.

The rest of me is on the other side of this site. I draw portraits for my friends' birthdays, which is basically my love language, and I'm deep enough into K-pop that the singing and dancing became real: performing with the Zijing Band, hosting as an MC, and dancing everything from Indonesian traditional at Gala Night to K-pop routines at the Student Festival. Drawing might feed the career one day. Singing and dancing are the safe space I run to when I need a break.
